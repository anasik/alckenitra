ETII Kenitra

ETII Teaching Internships

The English Teaching Internship Initiative (ETII) began as an offshoot of the ALC’s Community Service Club (CSC) in 2015. ALC volunteers were giving weekly English classes to students at a handicrafts center in Kenitra. However, with too many students and too few volunteers, the ALC’s CSC couldn’t keep up. Hence the idea was hatched to meet this need by bringing in English Master’s students from Sidi Mohamed Ben Abdellah University (SMBAU) to supplement the work of the ALC volunteers. This proved to be a win-win for everyone involved: 60 handicraft students were assured free weekly English lessons, and 4 Master’s students (many hoping to be English teachers one day) benefited from this unique professional development experience.

With the ETII model in place, in 2016 the ETII was awarded a $25,000 grant from the US State Department to test the model on a larger scale. As a part of this, ETII signed conventions with the local Ministry of Handicrafts and Entraide Nationale delegations, and SMBAU in order to create a legal framework for ETII. With the support of the US State Department and local Moroccan institutions, ETII was able to successfully grow out of the CSC into a separate entity, preparing a total of 30 ETII trainees in 2016-2017.

The ETII addresses two major interrelated challenges facing Moroccan youth, namely lack of access to education and lack of employable skills. ETII seeks to tackle these challenges by using English education as a means of professional development, while pairing up two distinct groups of Moroccan youth to the benefit of each: young English BA holders and vocational training center students and staff. By pairing Moroccans English BA holders and vocational training center students and staff together, ETII offers a simple solution to provide access to education and employable skills to each group.

The English BA holders are offered a free, high quality 60-70-hour course in English language pedagogy, in addition to other professional development opportunities. Following this, ETII trainees are dispatched as interns to vocational training centers where they sharpen their English teaching skills and develop their leadership and problem-solving capacities. In doing so, ETII provides its trainees with an opportunity to teach under-privileged students with limited access to English language education, enhancing the latter students’ employment prospects in return.

With a successful pilot program year behind it, ETII and the parent organization of the ALCs, the American Cultural Association (ACA), were uniquely positioned in 2017-2018 to expand ETII operations nationwide. With the model, the partnerships, the contacts and the network already in place, in 2017-2018 ETII replicated the success it enjoyed in Kenitra in 6 other communities in Morocco (Kenitra, Oujda, Meknes, Tangier, Kenitra, Rabat, and Casablanca), increasing the amount of ETII trainees from 30 to 79. In 2018-2019, ETII continued its operations in these seven cities, while starting an ETII program in El Jadida too, bringing the total of ETII trainees to 90.
